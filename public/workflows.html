01 · The pattern behind every workflow

Most AI automations fail quietly: they pad, invent, or drift, and people stop trusting them. Every workflow I build follows the same six-step shape, with two checkpoints that decide whether the output is worth trusting.

1 · Trigger

A schedule, an event, or a request. Each run starts fresh.

2 · Gather

Research, files or screens: the raw input.

3 · Verify

Anything that can't be traced to a source is dropped.

→
4 · Design

A designed output with a fixed structure, not a wall of text.

→
5 · Check

The output is looked at, by the agent or a person, before it ships.

→
6 · Deliver

To where people already are. "Nothing today" is a valid result.

Daily AI Updates

Running · weekdays

A card report of what's genuinely new in AI: tools that launched, features the major labs shipped, and research worth knowing. It reaches my inbox and my phone by 7:00.

Trigger

07:00 IST, Mon–Fri. Monday covers the weekend.

→
Research

Web search and reading for the last 24–72 hours.

→
Filter

8–12 new items. Rumours with no real source are cut.

→
Design

Card grid: title, category, "why it matters", source.

→
Deliver

Page by email, plus a short summary that leads with the day's biggest item.

Design decision: on a genuinely quiet day, it says so. A feed that pads teaches people to skim, and then they miss the day that matters.

03 · On demand — 7 AI pipelines across the design lifecycle

These run when the work calls for them. Blue steps are where a person decides; everything else the AI does.

AI stepHuman decision
Research

AI UX research partner

In use · walk-through

It covers the full research loop, but its first move is to question the brief: what decision will this inform, and what would change our mind? Every finding carries its evidence and a confidence level.

1Frame the question→2Choose method→3Plan & sample→4Write guide / script→5Run sessions→6Code & synthesise→7Decide what it means
Testing

Synthetic usability testing

Live

A cheap first pass that shows where a redesign is likely to fail, so real research is aimed where it matters. Results are measured from simulated sessions, with confidence intervals.

1Upload before / after→2Analyse flows→3Generate persona panel→4Lock the panel→5Simulated sessions→6KPI matrix + report→7Verify with real users
Try it ↗
Storytelling

Case-study pipeline — five specialist skills

In use · walk-through

Instead of one prompt that writes a case study, five specialists each do one job, the way a design director, writer and information designer would split it. I decide the story; they do the heavy lifting.

1Pick the story & audience→2Narrative architect: structure→3Template builder: fit to project type→4Narrative writer: prose→5Data visualiser: charts→6Diagram architect: journeys, blueprints→7Edit & approve
Build

Figma ↔ code through MCP

Walk-through

Figma is connected to Claude Code through MCP, so a frame becomes working code and changes can travel back to Figma. Design and build stop drifting apart after handoff.

1Design the frame→2Read design context via MCP→3Generate code with real tokens→4Sync changes back to Figma→5Review in both
Quality

Parity — handoff QA against a contract

Walk-through

Built for IBM HMC design QA. Design and engineering agree a handoff contract up front, and the build is checked against it in a validation console, so mismatches are caught by the tool, not argued about in review.

1Agree the contract→2Build ships→3Validate against contract→4Mismatch list→5Fix or accept
Teaching

Course pipeline — from topic to cohort

In progress

The nine-stage pipeline I'm using to build my course on AI-native design workflow. It is backward-designed from what a designer should be able to do at the end.

1Offer & promise→2Audience research synthesis→3Backward-designed curriculum→4Lessons & exercises→5Diagrams→6Posts from the lessons→7Sales prep→8Post-cohort revision
Conversation

Portfolio agent — never a blank error

Live

Answers visitors' questions from my documented work. If the first model is unavailable it falls back to a second, then to an offline index, so a recruiter always gets an answer.

1Question→2GPT-4o mini→3Fallback model→4Offline index→5Answer + matching case study
