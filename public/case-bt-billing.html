01 · FRAMING

The portal isn't broken. The system behind it is.

Read three days of Trustpilot reviews and the same shape appears. "Spinning wheel." "Phantom charges." "The chatbot keeps asking about my router when I'm trying to dispute £719." Every one is a real, daily indignity — and every one is downstream of the same thing: BT's billing service is stitched together from subsystems that don't share a source of truth.

So this was never "make the bill prettier." It was make the service legible.

Working hypothesis · W1If we rebuild around a single source of truth, portal completion jumps 12% → 78% and calls fall by half — because 3 of the 5 top pains are integration failures wearing UI clothing.
Fig 01
Five symptoms → three measurable failures → one root cause. Drawn on day 4, after the third re-read of the review corpus.
Method · thematic coding of 120K+ Trustpilot reviews; top-quartile of 1★ reviews tagged "billing", "portal", "chatbot".
Why this mattersDiagnoses that stop at "the UI is bad" produce redesigns that don't survive contact with the backend. Naming the root cause is what turns a visual refresh into a service redesign with teeth.

03 · ECOSYSTEM

A bill dispute touches five rings of actors. None of them currently agree.

To redesign a service you have to know who pulls the levers — and who's blocking which. This took a week of drawing and re-drawing: v1 was hierarchical (wrong — these actors aren't ranked), v2 was a network (too dense), v3 is a concentric radial. Each ring is owned by a different internal team with a different KPI. That's why there's no source of truth.

Fig 05–06
Three roles in the top-right own this. Marketing isn't among them — the most important thing the influence grid says. Positioning came from an org-chart audit, public statements and role-tracking of named exec roles.
If I'd known the influence map would take six hours to draw, I'd have started it on day 1, not day 6. Lesson learned.
Why this mattersEvery enterprise redesign I've seen fail in 15 years failed because the designer didn't earn the trust of the three roles in the top-right before they earned the brief.

The fights I'm proud of losing well

Conflict · W7

Billing wanted to keep the PDF invoice

Their position: "It's the legal record." My evidence: 9/15 test users never opened it; it was the #2 source of "phantom charge" confusion. Resolution: kept the PDF as a download, but made the plain-English explainer the default. The user won.

Conflict · W8

Marketing fought pre-bill alerts

Their position: "Another notification customers will mute." My call: opt-out, frequency-adjustable — I held the line. Where I gave ground: auto-escalation; I wanted 1 chatbot loop before a human, I got 2. A smaller win is still a win.

Fig 13a
The intervention that mattered most wasn't a screen — it was an operating model. Before, three teams each owned a fragment and none owned the outcome. The fix: a governance group with one shared metric. Org design is design.

04 · RESEARCH

Three personas. One shared injury: a service that doesn't trust them with the truth.

120,000+ Trustpilot reviews. 8,400 Resolver disputes. 600+ forum threads. Three weeks of coding. Eighty quotes pulled. These personas aren't composites of interviews — they're derived from the public record, which means anyone can audit the work. That's more rigorous than most persona-building, not less.

A name and a job title is a demographic. What predicts behaviour is the job someone is hiring the bill to do — so I read three behaviours, not three profiles.

The reconciler

Marcus · finance

Verifies every charge, keeps audit evidence, escalates anomalies. Primary persona — his pain touches every system failure at once.

The delegator

Sarah · ops

Wants minimal involvement: task done fast, then gone.

The evaluator

Diane · owner

Compares options, weighs switching cost, watches the contract clock.

07 · SYSTEM

A design system is just a set of compressed decisions.

Each principle exists because something in the old portal violated it. Each trade-off was an argument with engineering, legal or marketing — and the fights matter more than the wins.

6 principles · each with a "we will / we won't"

Principle 01

Explain every number

✓ plain-English on every line✗ never hide behind PDFs
Principle 02

Notify before, never after

✓ alert 5 days pre-change✗ surprise the customer
Principle 03

Leaving = joining

✓ downgrade as visible as upgrade✗ hide cancel behind chatbot
Principle 04

Context travels with the customer

✓ case state preserved across surfaces✗ ask them to re-explain
Principle 05

Humans within 2 steps

✓ auto-escalate after 2 loops✗ trap users in dead ends
Principle 06

Support is a feature, not a cost

✓ treat support as service design✗ optimise deflection above all

Decision log · 5 hard trade-offs

Fight #1 · W6 · Won

Auto-create dispute case, or confirm?

Auto-create (silent), rollback within 24h. Cost: higher false-positive load on triage. Low cost, big convenience.

Fight #2 · W6 · Held

Retention offers during cancellation?

Yes, but once and skippable in one click. Marketing wanted multi-step; I held the line. Honest compromise.

Fight #3 · W7

Unified data layer or API adapters?

Unified layer in phase 3; adapters for phases 1–2. Phase 3 is the long pole — budget exposure if it slips.

Fight #4 · W7 · Lost

Chatbot: kill or keep?

Wanted to kill it. Legal said no. Got: keep, auto-escalate after 2 loops, never deflect. The loss I think about most.

Fight #5 · W8 · Held

Pre-bill alerts: opt-in or opt-out?

Opt-out, frequency adjustable. Marketing pushed back hard on "another notification." Held the line.

"I lost the fight on auto-escalation timing. Wanted 1 loop. Got 2. Still a win — just a smaller one."— decision log, week 8
Why this mattersA design system without a decision log is a styleguide. A decision log without losses is a hagiography. Show the costs — the wins read truer.

5 findings from testing (3 rounds, n=35)

Finding 01

Explainers used by 15/15

Even people who said they "wouldn't need it." Not optional.

Finding 02

"Compare to last month" most-clicked

Users want anchoring before detail.

Finding 03

9/15 copied the dispute case ID

For their own records, not BT's. A trust gap.

Finding 04

2 tried to share dispute by email

Future affordance: shareable case URL with auth.

Finding 05

13/15 cancelled despite retention offer

Retention exists where it helps — not as friction.

"I can see exactly what changed and I can question it without phoning. That's all I've ever wanted."— v3 prototype participant, Marcus persona

What I learned as a design leader

What I underestimated

How much of this was an organisational problem, not a UX one. The hardest "redesign" was getting three teams to share one metric.

What I'd do differently

Bring legal into discovery, not sign-off. Secure an engineering sponsor before concept work. Validate assumptions before the team falls in love with a direction.

The surprise: most of what looked like UX debt was governance debt in a UX costume. I'd lead the next one from the operating model down, not the screen up.
Why this mattersA redesign isn't a deliverable — it's a wager that the diagnosis was right, sponsors hold, and engineering lands. Showing the wager (terms, odds, costs) separates portfolio work from a press release.
