Synthetic usability testing

UX KPI Testing Lab

Live

Usability research is slow and usually happens too late to change a decision. The Lab predicts where a redesign will fail before it reaches users. You upload before-and-after screens and build a persona panel. Each persona then expands into a group of simulated participants who attempt the task.

The design choice I care most about: every number is measured from those simulated sessions, not guessed by a single prompt. That means success rates come with confidence intervals, and a before-versus-after difference can be traced to the design change. The report says clearly that these are predictions to check with real users.

Proactive AI assistant

Buddy — an assistant that earns the right to interrupt

Live demo

Proactive assistants usually fail the same way: they interrupt too often, and people learn to ignore them. Buddy treats every nudge as a claim that has to earn attention. A trust engine scores each possible insight on how direct its source is, how established the evidence is, how accurate that life area has been, and what's at stake. It then sorts the insight into one of four tiers: silent, passive, ambient or proactive. Only the top tier interrupts you.

Confirming or dismissing an insight retunes trust for that area of life. Claude writes the daily briefing from whatever clears the bar. Voice runs on the device (Whisper to listen, Kokoro to speak), so what you say doesn't leave your device just to be heard.

What to look at
  1. On Home, the daily plan shows what Buddy chose to surface, and what it held back.
  2. Open Today and Life to see nudges grouped by life domain.
  3. Tap the mic to open Ask Buddy.

03 · Designing an AI product where trust is the whole product

Voice agent · elderly care

Saanjh — a companion that is read like an instrument

Design concept

Voice is the right way in for an older adult: nothing to learn, no fine motor control needed. But when older adults were asked about the voice agents built for them, their words were "childish," "patronizing" and "shallow." The failure was one of tone, not technology. Saanjh is designed against that failure.

One agent, one conversation, four readings — not four features

WhyThe obvious build is a companion, a pill reminder, a safety monitor and a family dashboard. That creates four modes, and modes break voice interfaces because there's no screen to show which one you're in.
Trade-offOne warm voice she simply talks to. Four reads run quietly over the same conversation: wellbeing, clinical, risk, and the shape of her speech. None of them can act on its own.
ResultHealth and safety signals come from the conversation she's already having, so she's never asked twice and never spoken to as a patient.

She is told before anyone else is

WhySilent reporting teaches her the machine is on someone else's side. After that, she stops speaking freely and the signal is gone.
Trade-offFamilies ask for transcripts. They get a 40-word weekly summary and one thing to do instead. If she refuses to share something, that refusal is respected, and the family only learns that a boundary exists.
ResultProtecting the conversation is protecting the data. Only the family view loses detail by design; she never loses her privacy.
L0–L1A single observation, or one worth a second look. Nobody is told, and the conversation carries on normally.
L2A pattern over two or more days. The agent asks her directly and suggests something she can do herself.
L3–L4The pattern needs a person. At L3 the agent asks her permission first; at L4 it tells her it is calling.
L5Life-threatening. The only level with no consent step, and it is scoped as narrowly as possible.

05 · Design operations — how I run the work

A design team is a production system. I run it the way I'd design any system: make the work visible, put decisions where the knowledge is, and measure the flow, not just the output.

Visibility

Progress reporting as a design tool

On IBM PowerVC I ran six months of work across five parallel workstreams from one tracker. It paired screen output with research and quality signals, on a monthly audit rhythm. It showed what was complete, what needed to converge, and where dependencies were piling up.

Evidence: PowerVC case · 72+ screens · feedback rounds 2.5× Jan→Jun
Decision rights

Governance instead of a bottleneck

On IBM Power Systems, I put 3-in-a-box governance (design, product and engineering deciding together) and a fixed critique cadence in place of a central approval queue. Four squads converged on one design language without slowing down.

Evidence: Power Systems case · +35% first-pass approvals · ~20% less UI drift
Quality at handoff

Parity: agree the contract, then check it

Design QA used to be a debate at the end. With Parity, the design and the build are checked against an agreed handoff contract in a validation console, so a mismatch is caught by the tool before it becomes a meeting.

Status: internal tool · walk-through on request
Measurement

Complexity measured, not argued

I built a UX complexity analysis tool based on IBM's Sobiesiak/O'Keefe method, so a debate about whether a flow is "too complex" becomes a score the team can compare before and after a change.

Status: internal tool · walk-through on request

The trade-off I made openly: delivery breadth over research depth

WhyFive workstreams had to keep shipping, and formal discovery time was the scarce resource.
Trade-offI ran H1 on one formal discovery session and logged the research gap as an explicit watch item for H2, rather than hiding it.
ResultThat gap is exactly why I invested in the AI research tooling below: making research cheap enough that it stops being the thing we trade away.

Why this matters for a team

These are personal today, but the pattern is what I'd bring to a team: a scheduled trigger, a research step, a verification gate, a designed output, and an honest "nothing today". The same shape works for a weekly competitor scan, a research-repository digest, or a design-debt report.

07 · AI for research — faster, without getting sloppier

Its first job is to question the research question: what decision will this inform, what do we already believe, and what evidence would change our mind? 08 · Where I draw the line

01Synthetic users predict; they don't replace. The Lab tells you where to point real research. It never stands in for it.
02No invented numbers. If a target isn't known yet, I leave the gap visible and set it with the team.
03An agent should never claim feelings it doesn't have. Honesty is what keeps the relationship intact when the system gets something wrong.
04Consent before disclosure. An AI system that reports about people must tell them first.
05Label what's real. Live, code, concept or walk-through, on every item on this page.
