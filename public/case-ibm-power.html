LEADERSHIP CASE STUDY · IBM · POWER SYSTEMS

Unified four squads around one design language.

Product Design Manager. Four product squads were drifting into four dialects of the same interface. I built the design system and the operating model around it — governance, decision rights and critique — so the squads converged on one language without slowing delivery.

My mandate
Establish a shared product language across four squads
Role
Product Design Manager

12 months
Ownership

Outcome

01 · Context and stakes

Power Systems shipped across four squads, each moving fast and each solving interface problems its own way. The result was mounting inconsistency: divergent patterns, duplicated components and rework in critique. The stakes were delivery speed and trust — a heavier central process could fix consistency but would create a design bottleneck the squads would route around.

02 · The hardest decision

Govern the system, don't centralize the work

WhyA central design gate would have restored consistency but slowed every squad and eroded ownership.
Trade-offI invested in tokens, shared patterns and clear decision rights instead of approvals — slower to show visible screens, harder to sell than a mockup.
ResultSquads kept their pace; consistency became a shared standard, not a queue.

03 · How I led the team

I managed four designers — coaching, career planning and feedback — while partnering with product and engineering leads across the squads. I set the critique cadence and decision rights so contributions to the system were reviewed consistently, delegated ownership of pattern areas to individual designers, and kept executive stakeholders aligned on why governance beat a central bottleneck. Conflicts were resolved at the level of the standard, not the individual screen.
