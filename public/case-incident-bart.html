The slowest part of incident response was the part before anyone responded.

An on-call team was losing its most expensive minutes — the first five — to five disconnected tools just to start. I designed BART: an assistant that collapses report, alert, and war-room into a single Microsoft Teams conversation, while ServiceNow quietly stays the system of record.

The brief said “build a faster form.” The form ranked last.

A process audit showed the cost lived in everything around the form — re-keying into Slack, blasting email, hunting for the bridge. I named it the fragmentation tax and reframed the brief from speeding up intake to compressing the first five minutes of coordination.

Five tool-hops and a mental-model gap collapsed into four design moves

The fragmentation tax (red) — every manual handoff between noticing and resolving — clusters into four interventions (lime) that removed the coordination cost (green).

A five-phase sprint, two-thirds spent understanding before any pixels

In incident response a wrong information model costs more than a wrong colour — so framing and structure led. Two forks settled the shape of the product.

So whatFront-loading the model was the leadership call. Shipping all three invocation paths — not one — meant every role had a natural way in.
